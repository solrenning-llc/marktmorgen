## The requirement (binding, AGENTS.md SR5, applied from the owner's report of Stripe's answer of 27 Oct 2026)

- A **real US business street address**, never a P.O. box.
- The registered agent's address should not be the operating address (not recommended).
- A virtual office is not guaranteed to be accepted; if automatic verification fails, the account goes to manual review.
- Stripe Atlas partners offer a discounted US address. No quote or acceptance may be presumed.
- The US bank account must be in the LLC's exact name.

## What Stripe's own pages say (read 13 Nov 2026, 02:57 UTC)

From https://support.stripe.com/questions/which-address-should-i-use-for-atlas-incorporation, verbatim:

- "If you have a U.S. physical address — You can use it everywhere — it works for every step of incorporation, Stripe payments, and banking."
- "If you don't have a U.S. physical address — You may need a virtual U.S. address for some steps. You can get one through our Atlas partner Stable."
- "Incorporation: Any address works, U.S. or non-U.S., except your registered agent address."
- "**Stripe payments: You can use a physical or virtual U.S. address.**"
- "**Banking: Most banks require a physical address.** Stripe Treasury and some banking partners accept non-U.S. physical addresses."
- "Can I use my registered agent address? No. … Don't use this address for incorporation or banking."

From https://support.stripe.com/questions/verifying-your-business-address, verbatim:

- "Stripe requires customers to complete a Know Your Customer (KYC) process which includes verifying their business address with required documentation such as rental agreements, utility bills and tax returns. We may ask for extra documentation to verify addresses that cannot be automatically verified without it."
- "KYC obligations require Stripe to verify that account holders are operating from the business addresses they provide."
- Acceptable documents: rental or lease agreement; utility bills; federal or state tax returns; Articles of Incorporation or other government-issued business documents. Each must show the business's name or the account owner's name.

From https://support.stripe.com/questions/how-can-i-forward-my-mail-to-my-company-address-outside-of-the-u-s, verbatim: "we would recommend using a mail forwarding service. Atlas users can access discounts at two providers: Stable offers Stripe Atlas users 3 months free via a link in the Atlas application. LegalZoom Virtual Mail offers Stripe Atlas users a 30% discount." The same page: the registered agent "renewal fee is $100", yearly.

From https://support.stripe.com/questions/connect-address-validation: "KYC obligations require Stripe to verify that account holders are operating from the business addresses they provide."

**What this means, and what it does not.** Stripe's text accepts a *virtual* US address for Stripe payments, and the only provider it names for that address is Stable. It does not use the words "CMRA" or "P.O. box" on these pages, and it does not promise that any particular address passes: an address that cannot be verified automatically needs a document (lease, utility bill, tax return or a government business document) in the LLC's or the owner's name. A mailbox provider does not issue utility bills or leases, so for a virtual address the document would most likely be a government document showing that address (for example the formation filing or an IRS letter); that is this study's reading, **not** a Stripe statement. This is consistent with SR5: a real street address, never a P.O. box, and no guaranteed acceptance of a virtual office. For the bank, Stripe's own text says most banks require a physical address, so a virtual mailbox address may be refused by the bank even where Stripe accepts it; Stripe adds that Stripe Treasury and some banking partners accept a non-US physical address (here, the owner's address in Fortaleza), without naming a bank or guaranteeing eligibility.

## One-page recommendation for the owner (Depende do dono)

**No option meets every requirement with a dated source.** For every provider, the Form 1583/CMRA status and the US bank's acceptance are unknown, so by the repository's rule each fails until read. Within that limit:

1. **Safest if Stripe's acceptance is the main doubt: Stable Grow, yearly, $49/month = $588/year at list** ($708/year if billed monthly). It is the only provider Stripe's own text names for a virtual US address, and Stripe says it accepts "a physical or virtual U.S. address" for Stripe payments. Possible reductions, none counted until confirmed at checkout: 50% off Grow under $1 million revenue or when incorporating with a Stable address (→ $294/year), 3 months free through the Atlas link, $200 off Atlas for Stable customers. Allowance: 360 pieces a year, then $3 each.
2. **Cheapest at list price: LegalZoom Virtual Mail Starter, $39/month = $468/year** (30% off for Atlas users per Stripe → $327.60/year if it lasts the whole year; duration not stated). It is a real street address, but Stripe names it only as a mail-forwarding service, not as the payments address, and it scans only 5 items a month without fee.
3. **Bank:** Stripe's text says most banks require a physical address, so neither virtual address is shown to work for the bank. Stripe says Stripe Treasury and some banking partners accept a non-US physical address (the owner's own address in Brazil), only for whoever is eligible. The bank account must still be in the LLC's exact name (SR5).
4. **Never** use the registered agent's address for Stripe payments or the bank (Stripe's text and SR5).

The single difference between the two, $120 a year at list, buys the only explicit mention by Stripe. This study's choice, for the owner to decide, is **Stable Grow yearly**, keeping in the cost model the list $588/year (the discounts are unknown until confirmed, so they count as not given), plus the unknown Form 1583 cost, which keeps the result failing until it is read.
