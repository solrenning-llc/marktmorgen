## Primary classification and customer-location evidence

The National Tax Agency's [July 2024 cross-border electronic-services brochure](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/consumption_tax/0024006-219.pdf) says electronic services include paid digital content, software, cloud use, storage and other listed internet supplies. Whether a supply is domestic is determined from the recipient's whereabouts. The brochure calls for objective and reasonable customer-location criteria suited to the transaction; its example compares a customer-supplied address with the country of issue of the payment card. This evidence does not add such collection or decide which evidence is sufficient.

The same brochure separates B2B electronic services, for which the domestic recipient generally uses the reverse charge, from other electronic services treated as B2C. Classification turns on the nature and terms of the service. Merely labelling an open web service "business use" does not make it B2B when consumers cannot effectively be excluded. This document does not classify Marktmorgen's supplies or customers, and it does not treat a buyer's label as conclusive.

For a foreign business's B2C electronic services, the July 2024 brochure says the foreign provider files and pays unless platform taxation applies. The [NTA platform page](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/consumption_tax/05.htm) and its [foreign-business Q&A](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/consumption_tax/PFQA_en_foreign.pdf), both read on the evidence date, describe the April 1, 2025 rule. It shifts filing and payment to a specified platform business only when the foreign business supplies B2C electronic services through its digital platform and the compensation is received through that specified platform. A direct supply, direct receipt or an undesignated platform is not made platform-taxed merely because software or a marketplace is involved. Actual contracting, collection and designated-platform facts remain unverified.

## Liability, agent, return and payment work

The July 2024 brochure describes a general exemption threshold of 10 million yen of taxable sales in the base period and says B2B electronic-service sales are excluded from the example calculation for a foreign electronic-services-only business. The [NTA basic-knowledge page](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/consumption_tax/01.htm) separately explains the base period, taxable-person routes and foreign-currency conversion: in principle, each non-JPY transfer is converted using the market T.T.M. on the transfer date. Those summaries do not determine the LLC's base period, taxable sales, currency method, voluntary elections or qualified invoice status.

The brochure also says a foreign corporation without a head office or office in Japan must designate a resident tax agent when the described filing obligations apply. Becoming a qualified invoice issuer is a separate voluntary decision in that guidance and can itself make the business taxable regardless of base-period sales. This evidence does not appoint a tax agent or select a filing route, invoice registration, office, adviser or election.

The [NTA general filing-and-payment page](https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shohi/6601.htm), stated for law as of April 1, 2026, says a taxable corporation generally files and pays for each taxable period within two months after the period ends; a corporation's ordinary taxable period follows its business year. Payment is generally due on the same date. Extensions and interim returns have separate conditions. The page also states a narrow no-sales and no-tax-due condition under which a final return need not be filed, while a refund return may still be possible when input tax or interim payment exists. That is not a universal nil-return rule and does not establish the LLC's actual account calendar. Every applicable period, due date, payment and receipt would need current confirmation.

## Records and unresolved qualifications

The [NTA recordkeeping page](https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shohi/6621.htm), stated for law as of April 1, 2026, describes general consumption-tax books for taxable businesses. It lists the counterparty, transaction date, transaction content and amount separated by rate among the entries, subject to stated exceptions. It describes a seven years retention period measured from two months after the relevant taxable-period end; when the corporate filing-extension special rule applies, that start is three months after the period end.

Those general rules are not a deletion schedule.
